Arm placing objects

Problem

The end of the arm has to reach poses fixed on the field. The robot is a swerve chassis with a three-joint planar arm: shoulder, telescope and wrist. Instead of hand-writing arm setpoints and state machines, a solver on the coprocessor should decide how the whole robot moves, and the robot controller should only close the control loops.

What I built

I wrote both halves. On the coprocessor, a Python ROS 2 node runs a phase state machine and, every tick, a quadratic program (QP) built with Placo over a model of the whole robot, under hard limits and weighted soft goals. On the robot, a WPILib joint executor hands the newest joint targets to the motor controllers’ position loops and reports the measured joints back.

How it works

The phases run DRIVE, APPROACH, TASK, RETRACT and back to DRIVE; the robot never sees the phase. APPROACH waits until the arm reaches the goal’s aim posture and the chassis its staging pose. On entering TASK, the goal is converted into the solver’s frame through the chassis pose measured at that moment, so it stays at the same field point. Speed limits bound each QP step, so the joint targets [q1, d, q3] walk toward the goal over many ticks as they stream to the robot over UDP. The two streams fail in opposite ways on purpose: a lost arm target leaves the arm holding the last one, while a chassis command older than 0.3 s stops the chassis. Driving to the staging pose and gating TASK on arrival were validated on hardware; the QP moving the chassis in TASK was tested in simulation only.
