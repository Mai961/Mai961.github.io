Localizing the arm’s field-fixed goal

Problem

The arm’s goals are poses fixed on the field, while the whole-body solver works in the robot’s own frame. So the robot has to know where it stands. AprilTags, printed squares at known places on the field, give that pose, but a single flat tag has a two-way pose ambiguity.

What I built

The localizer that turns tag sightings into a field pose, and the whole-body controller that uses it. The localizer started from a teammate’s port of an existing package; I developed its pooled multi-tag solve, the weighted refine and the covariance. The whole-body node reads the robot’s field pose, which the field EKF (extended Kalman filter) builds from those fixes, directly over ROS 2.

How it works

The localizer is described on the camera-stream page; here it matters only that each fix carries a covariance the EKF can weight. The phases run DRIVE, APPROACH, TASK and RETRACT. APPROACH aims the arm, then waits until it reaches the goal’s aim posture. It also waits until a fresh field pose shows the chassis at its staging pose. When the arm enters TASK, the whole-body node converts the field-fixed goal into the solver’s frame through the chassis pose measured at that moment. Each QP step then moves the end of the arm toward it within joint and speed limits. Freezing the field-fixed goal at TASK entry was tested in simulation; hardware validation was still pending.
