Arm tracking an AprilTag

Problem

The arm’s goals are poses fixed on the field, while the whole-body solver works in the robot’s own frame, so the robot has to know where it stands. AprilTags, printed squares at known places on the field, give that pose, but a single flat tag has a two-way pose ambiguity.

What I built

The localizer that turns tag sightings into a field pose, and the whole-body controller that uses it. The localizer started from a teammate’s port of an existing package; I developed its pooled multi-tag solve, the weighted refine and the covariance. The whole-body node reads the robot’s field pose, which the field EKF builds from those fixes, directly over ROS 2.

How it works

With two or more tags in view, the localizer pools all their corners into one solve, each weighted by its tag’s apparent edge length squared, so small, distant tags fade out smoothly. OpenCV’s SQPnP seeds the pose, and a weighted Levenberg-Marquardt refine improves it. The covariance comes from a numeric Jacobian; if the geometry is not observable, the pose is withheld instead of inventing a covariance. The field EKF weights each fix by that covariance. When the arm enters TASK, the whole-body node converts the field-fixed goal into the solver’s frame through the chassis pose measured at that moment, and each QP step moves the end of the arm toward it within joint and speed limits. Freezing the field-fixed goal at TASK entry was tested in simulation; hardware validation was still pending.
