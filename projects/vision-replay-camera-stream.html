AprilTag localizer, with the camera’s recorded point cloud

Problem

The field EKF (extended Kalman filter) downstream weights each tag fix by its covariance instead of accepting or rejecting it with thresholds. So the localizer has to report a covariance it actually computed. It also works across two scales: field coordinates reach about 17 m, while the tags are centimetres across.

What I built

The AprilTag localizer, a C++ ROS 2 node that turns a camera image into the camera’s pose on the field plus a 6x6 covariance. It started from a teammate’s port of an existing package; the pooled multi-tag solve, its weighted refine and the covariance are mine. The node never opens a camera; it reads an image topic, which on the robot came from the Odin camera’s own driver. The clip’s point cloud is the driver’s own output, not mine.

How it works

Each detection first passes acceptance gates: decode quality, an allow-list, an edge margin and range. With two or more tags, all corners are pooled, and the field points are recentred on their centroid. Then OpenCV’s SQPnP (a perspective-n-point solver) seeds the pose. A weighted Levenberg–Marquardt (a least-squares refinement) step follows. It keeps the seed if the result is non-finite, puts a point behind the camera, or fits worse. With one tag, IPPE (OpenCV’s planar-target solver) returns both candidate poses of the flat square. The covariance gains the spread toward the second pose, weighted by a flip rate fitted to recorded data. The node publishes the camera pose with its covariance, each tag solved alone, and every detection, including rejected ones and why.
