Vision replay: the camera’s image stream

Problem

The field EKF downstream weights each tag fix by its covariance instead of accepting or rejecting it with thresholds, so the localizer has to report a covariance it actually computed. It also works across two scales: field coordinates reach about 17 m, while the tags are centimetres across.

What I built

The AprilTag localizer, a C++ ROS 2 node that turns a camera image into the camera’s pose on the field plus a 6x6 covariance. It started from a teammate’s port of an existing package; the pooled multi-tag solve, its weighted refine and the covariance are mine. The node never opens a camera; it reads an image topic, which on the robot came from the Odin camera’s own driver.

How it works

Each detection first passes acceptance gates: decode quality, an allow-list, an edge margin and range. With two or more tags, all corners are pooled, and the field points are recentred on their centroid before OpenCV’s SQPnP seeds the pose. A weighted Levenberg-Marquardt refine follows, and keeps the seed if the result is non-finite, puts a point behind the camera, or fits worse. With one tag, IPPE returns both candidate poses of the flat square, and the covariance gains the spread toward the second pose, weighted by a flip rate fitted to recorded data. The node publishes the camera pose with its covariance, each tag solved alone to check the field layout, and every detection, including rejected ones and why.
